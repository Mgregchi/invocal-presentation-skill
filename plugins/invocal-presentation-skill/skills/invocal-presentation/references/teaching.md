# Teaching and visual direction

## Research and lesson structure

Use authoritative sources suited to the topic: primary research/official documentation for technical material, clinical authorities for medicine, reliable original data for quantitative claims. Verify facts that can change. Preserve dates, populations, units, limitations and source links. Explain uncertainty honestly. For high-stakes subjects, distinguish education from individualized professional advice and do not invent expert approval.

Start with a motivating question or realistic scenario and at most four or five clear objectives. Build explanations in an order that equips the learner for the next concept. Teach terminology rather than listing it. Use examples, comparisons or evidence where they clarify a mechanism. End with practical takeaways that answer the opening question. Match complexity to the audience and avoid unnecessary jargon.

Write complete narration before deciding slide durations. Use sections short enough to regenerate independently. Preserve exact scripts. Make visible text concise; let narration supply detail. Do not force an arbitrary duration by rushing the voice or truncating ideas. If a target length is too short for the requested coverage, adjust depth explicitly or explain the tradeoff.

## Visuals

Prefer accurately sourced diagrams, medical illustrations, original datasets and meaningful photographs. Record creator, source URL, license and adaptations. Source-page ownership is not blanket permission for every image. Inspect attribution for privately copyrighted images on official sites. Avoid generating evidence, precise anatomical structures or numerical charts with image synthesis.

Choose layout for the content. Use flat intentional composition, readable text, appropriate margins, varied arrangements and visual hierarchy. Avoid decorative panels, generic filler, fake graphs and tiny footnotes as the only location for important qualifications. Keep imagery undistorted and labels readable on a mobile screen. Keep relevant text, tables and charts editable through the host's presentation tools.

Establish a concept in narration, introduce its visual, reveal supporting material as it is discussed, emphasize the exact feature, and transition naturally. Choose unique word anchors and preserve semantic cues. Remove a previous pointer before changing focus. Keep notes, citations and attribution in the editable deck and scripts.

Use short purposeful transitions. A longer scene can remain coherent while its evidence and emphasis change. Do not add continuous motion solely to avoid a static-frame metric. Inspect any unchanged visual interval above roughly 15 seconds and decide whether it serves the explanation. There should be no accidental 30–40-second frozen slide while narration moves through unrelated ideas.

## Supported scope

The baseline is narration, sourced imagery, native editable text, timed cumulative reveals, focus outlines and short cross-dissolves. Rich charts, progressive plotting, continuous motion, camera paths, presenters and lip-sync are extensions. A host may support them, but validate each extension before claiming it works. Separate native presentation editability from video-only animation. Never promise that a rendered video effect automatically plays in PowerPoint.
